# From KPI-Driven Networks to Experience-Driven Networks

"The next generation of network optimization is not KPI-centric. It is human-centric."

For decades, telecom networks have been designed, monitored, and optimized around KPIs.
And for good reason.
KPIs provide measurable indicators of network health and operational performance.
Without them, managing a large-scale network would be nearly impossible.
However, as our industry evolves, a fundamental question emerges:

Are we optimizing what truly matters?
A network can achieve outstanding Accessibility, Retainability, Throughput, and Latency targets.
Yet customers may still perceive the service as unreliable.
Why?
Because KPIs describe network behavior.
Customers judge experiences.
This distinction is becoming increasingly important as operators face new challenges:

* Customer expectations continue to rise every year.
* New services demand more consistent performance.
* Network complexity is growing across RAN, transport, core, and cloud domains.
* Competitive differentiation increasingly depends on service quality rather than coverage alone.

In this environment, optimizing individual KPIs is no longer enough.
The objective must evolve from maximizing network metrics to maximizing customer outcomes.
This is where Experience-Driven Networks enter the conversation.
Instead of asking:
"Which KPI should improve?"

Future networks will ask:
"Which action will create the greatest benefit for users and the business?"

This requires a broader perspective.
Network KPIs remain essential.

But they become inputs rather than the final objective.
Customer experience, service assurance, business impact, and operational efficiency become part of the optimization target itself.
Technologies such as SON, SMO, AI, analytics, and closed-loop automation are accelerating this transition.

For the first time, operators have the opportunity to move beyond reactive optimization and toward intent-driven operations.
A future where networks continuously learn, adapt, and optimize based on customer needs rather than isolated technical metrics.

In my view, this is one of the most important shifts our industry will experience over the next decade.
Because ultimately, customers do not buy KPIs.
They buy connectivity, reliability, productivity, and trust.
And those are measured through experience.
